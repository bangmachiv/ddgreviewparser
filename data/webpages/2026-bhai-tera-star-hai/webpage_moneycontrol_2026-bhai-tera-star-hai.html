Bhai Tera Star Hai Movie Review: Raghav Juyal leads a comedy where the jokes need their own rescue mission

‘Bhai Tera Star Hai’ has a promising one-night comedy setup but gets trapped in its own web of confusion. Raghav Juyal brings energy, but weak writing ensures the laughs never truly arrive.

‘Bhai Tera Star Hai,’ directed by Vivek B Agrawal, released in theatres on 30 July and stars Raghav Juyal, Niki Walia, Sanjay Kapoor, Niharika NM, and Parvathy Omanakuttan.

A superstar dream meets a reality check

‘Bhai Tera Star Hai’ is a comedy that unfolds over one chaotic night in London. It follows a young man who is convinced that becoming a superstar is only a matter of time until a cricket bet turns his life upside down. The film has a decent setup and enough ingredients for a fun entertainer, but it never figures out how to make the most of them. The only thing that refuses to get lost is the confusion. The film clearly wants to bring back the flavour of old-school Bollywood comedies, where ordinary people found themselves trapped in ridiculous situations. However, nostalgia alone cannot replace sharp writing, and the movie struggles to create the warmth and madness that made those entertainers memorable. By the time the chaos settles, the film leaves behind little except the feeling of having wasted two hours.

One lie, too many problems

Ajay Singh (Raghav Juyal) is a struggling actor in London who is convinced that he is destined to become Bollywood’s next superstar. His dreams hit a roadblock when he loses money in a cricket bet and finds himself owing £10,000 to Fatty (Sanjay Kapoor). With very little time to arrange the money, Ajay tells a lie about his sister Natasha (Parvathy Omanakuttan), setting off a chain of misunderstandings. The lie pulls his friends Sid (Vivan Bhatena), JD, their girlfriends, sisters, and several others into a chaotic night involving failed plans, angry confrontations, gangsters and police intervention. What follows is a series of increasingly messy situations as Ajay struggles to escape the trouble he has created. The characters keep creating new problems so quickly that even the audience starts wondering if there is a reward for the person who can create the biggest mess.

When noise tries to replace comedy

The film misses the bus right from the beginning. Plots that deal with events happening in one night hold immense potential only if they are aided by great writing. Watching ‘Bhai Tera Star Hai’ gives a sense that the writers were more focussed on creating chaos than crafting comedy. A one-night comedy driven by misunderstandings can be great fun, but the screenplay keeps introducing new characters and side tracks without giving any of them enough purpose. Most of the jokes depend on loud arguments and repetitive situations instead of smart comic timing. The characters needed stronger personalities and sharper exchanges to make their chaos entertaining. Perhaps the makers believed that if everyone shouted loudly enough, the audience would mistake it for comedy. Even the London backdrop feels surprisingly ordinary and adds very little to the experience.

Raghav Juyal carries the burden

Raghav Juyal gives the film plenty of energy and remains committed even when the screenplay lets him down. His Ajay is designed as a dreamer who is completely convinced about his own greatness, and that self-belief could have created a genuinely funny character. Unfortunately, the screenplay rarely allows him to move beyond exaggerated reactions. Sanjay Kapoor brings some screen presence as Fatty, but his character never moves beyond being an angry man collecting money. Niharika NM, Niki Walia, Barkha Singh and the rest of the supporting cast are left with wafer-thin roles that rarely stand out. Chandan Roy Sanyal, playing the actor-hating policeman, delivers his part sincerely and manages to leave a better impression than most. At times, the actors seem to be searching for the script as much as the audience is searching for the laughs.

A star vehicle that runs out of fuel

‘Bhai Tera Star Hai’ wastes a promising idea with weak writing, forced humour and inconsistent direction. The performances are sincere, but they cannot rescue a screenplay that keeps going in circles without delivering enough laughs. Raghav Juyal has the natural ability to carry a comedy, but this film does not give him the material needed to showcase that talent fully. What could have been a breezy entertainer about ambition, friendship and chaos turns into an exhausting watch. The film wants to be a wild comedy ride, but unfortunately, it forgets to add the most important passenger: humour. There are a few amusing moments along the way, but not enough to justify a trip to the theatre.

Rating: 1.5/5
