Bhai Tera Star Hai Movie Review: A nightmare of a comedy that drags on

Ronak Kotecha
Jul 30, 2026, 9:00 AM IST

2.0/5

Story: On a cold night in London, wannabe superstar Ajay (Raghav Juyal) finds himself in deep trouble after losing a cricket bet to notorious club owner Fatty (Sanjay Kapoor). With just one night to repay the money, Ajay runs around the city spinning lies and creating chaos, only for the situation to spiral into a nightmare of epic proportions.Review: Stories that unfold over the course of a single night can make for incredibly engaging cinema. With a limited timeframe but endless possibilities, filmmakers across the world have experimented with this narrative style with varying degrees of success. Dancer and reality star turned actor Raghav Juyal's first solo outing as a leading man attempts to tap into this genre, but unfortunately misses the mark.The film follows Ajay, a young man desperate to become a superstar and constantly looking for shortcuts to wealth and fame. After losing a cricket bet, his life quickly descends into chaos. Rowdy club owner Fatty wants his money back, while Ajay's sister Natasha (Parvathy Omanakuttan) refuses to bail him out. At the same time, Natasha's evening with her new millionaire boyfriend is derailed by her obsessive ex Sid (Vivan Bhatena), who simply cannot move on. Ajay's girlfriend Roshni (Niharika NM), who also happens to be Sid's sister, gets dragged into the madness as she tries to rescue her boyfriend from the consequences of his own poor decisions.The screenplay is relentlessly chaotic and rarely makes much sense. Co-writer and director Vivek B. Agrawal's execution begins to lose steam very early, forcing the audience through what feels like an endlessly exhausting night filled with confusion rather than entertainment. Films built around such a premise thrive on sharp writing, clever misunderstandings and genuinely funny situations that snowball into bigger problems. Here, those moments are few and far between. Instead, almost every actor is loud, mouthing repetitive dialogue and most of the jokes simply fail to land. With too many characters crowding the narrative and an increasingly convoluted plot, it becomes difficult to stay invested in what is happening on screen.Raghav Juyal throws himself into the role, but often slips into overacting while portraying a young man trapped in an impossible situation. More nuanced writing and a far more restrained performance would have made Ajay a much easier character to root for. Vivan Bhatena shows considerably more restraint, but once again, the writing lets him down and prevents his scenes from leaving much of an impact.Character actors like Nikki Walia, Sanjay Kapoor and Chandan Roy Sanyal are left shouting, screaming and running around in the name of comedy. Roy Sanyal, in particular, feels completely wasted despite his proven acting calibre. Thankfully, the film finds some balance through its female characters. Niharika NM gets a handful of genuinely amusing lines, although her delivery remains average. Parvathy Omanakuttan emerges as one of the film's biggest positives with arguably the best-written character. Natasha is independent, level-headed and knows exactly how to deal with the emotionally immature men around her, whether it is her narcissistic brother or her clingy ex-boyfriend.The music by Amit Trivedi is enjoyable and adds some energy whenever it appears. However, it is rather puzzling to have two songs placed back-to-back after the end credits.‘Bhai Tera Star Hai’ tells the story of just one night, but ironically, it feels like a night that never ends. The premise has all the ingredients for an entertaining comedy and perhaps even a future cult favourite. Unfortunately, it lacks the genuinely funny characters, memorable dialogues and inventive comic situations needed to pull it off. What could have been a madcap comedy ends up being an exhausting watch that never quite finds its rhythm.
