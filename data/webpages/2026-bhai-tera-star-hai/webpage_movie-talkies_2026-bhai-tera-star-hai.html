Bhai Tera Star Hai Review: A painful blow to filmmaking standards –

Bhai Tera Star Hai is a comic caper directed by Vivek Agrawal. It stars Raghav Juyal, Sanjay Kapoor & others. Read our review below (Movie Talkies).

Sameer Ahire
31 Jul 2026 17:55 IST

Follow Us

New Update

RATING - ⭐ 1/5*

Bhai Tera Star Hai Review Movie Talkies:

I think I genuinely need to get my head examined after watching this movie. Am I okay? Am I even writing this right? Check, one, two, three… okay, perfect. Still, I might take a Saridon anyway to avoid the headache that’s clearly on its way. Bhai Tera Star Hai is more like, “Picture toh ekdam bekar hai.” How on earth did some people even have the guts to call this a film? This feels like one of those Instagram reel edits with flashy subtitles, animated fonts, and absolutely zero substance. Even film institute interns could have made something far better than this with their half-matured brains. This is next-level torture that will shatter your faith in filmmaking and, ironically, increase your respect for other bad films you’ve seen this year. Bhai Tera Star Hai exists only to make those films look better—and trust me, it works instantly. This is a painful blow to filmmaking standards and a severe attack on my understanding of cinema as a viewer. How I’m even going to end this review, only I know!

Bhai Tera Star Hai Story:

The film has a super messy story with a bunch of characters tangled into each other, but I’ll try to keep it short and simple. Ajay (Raghav Juyal) is a struggling actor who owes money to a bar owner, Fatty (Sanjay Kapoor). Fatty gives him a final deadline—midnight—to return the money. Ajay turns to his girlfriend Roshni (Niharika NM), who then approaches her brother Sid (Vivaan Bhatena). Sid, meanwhile, is roaming from bar to bar trying to recover money he once lent to his friends. One of them happens to be his ex-girlfriend Natasha (Parvathy Omanakuttam), who is also Ajay’s sister. However, Sid’s ego issues prevent him from taking money from her. Alongside him is Laila (Barkha Singh), trying to help him. The chain keeps going in circles until all characters finally land up at the same place—but by then, it’s already too late. Will Fatty get his money? Honestly, does it even matter?

The script here isn’t the main problem—the screenplay is. Everything is presented as if you’re explaining things to a five-year-old child. Those tacky subtitles, unnecessary thought templates, and illogical ideas go terribly wrong at every step. JD gets shot, but there’s no blood. He literally walks around with a bullet in his shoulder throughout the second half as if it’s nothing. Ajay’s IQ feels like it’s in the minus zone, and Sid’s ego is larger than only a pizza slice. There are too many exes, too many affairs, too many lies—and the entire screenplay turns into one big joke. A joke that neither makes you laugh nor allows you to walk away peacefully. Then comes a ridiculous climax that makes zero sense. And yes, there’s a remake song where the heroine flaunts her glamour in a wet look—but by then, no one is left in the theatre to even watch it. Most people have already walked out because they simply couldn’t tolerate it any longer.

Bhai Tera Star Hai Cast:

The performances are equally disappointing. Raghav Juyal was honestly better off not acting—this just isn’t his space. He delivers dialogues as if he’s reading off a teleprompter, barely even looking at his co-actors. Sanjay Kapoor looks stylish with his new hairdo, but in terms of acting? There’s barely anything. Niki Walia is decent, Vivan Bhatena goes overboard, Vikalp Mehta is below par, Tina Desai disappoints, and Niharika NM seems to be there only for glamour. Parvathy Omanakuttam looks stunning, and Barkha Singh mostly exists to sip martinis on screen. Overall, the entire cast contributes to making the experience even more exhausting.

"After Ramayana, don't do Animal": Mahabharata fame Nitish Bhardwaj advises Ranbir Kapoor –

Bhai Tera Star Hai Movie Review:

Technically, the film is just as weak. The cinematography is dull, the locations feel like they’ve been used just to claim rebates, the editing is sloppy, and the music is outright painful. The production design is somewhat decent, but the dialogues are terrible, to say the least. Vivek Agrawal’s direction ensures that you have a thoroughly unpleasant time at the movies. You’ll probably enjoy your popcorn more than the film—and might even walk out once the popcorn is finished. Bhai Tera Star Hai is a serious strike against those who genuinely believe in cinema and storytelling. This is not just bad—it’s unbearable. Easily one of the worst films of the year, and if there were an award for delivering wholesome torture, this one would win it unanimously.

7 Dogs Review: Not just the film, even the cameos of Salman Khan & Sanjay Dutt are bad

7 Dogs is a South Arabian action thriller directed by Adil and Bilal. The film stars Ahmed Ezz, Kareem Adbel Aziz, Salman Khan and Sanjay Dutt. Read our review below (Movie Talkies).

Sameer Ahire
22 Aug 2026 18:46 IST

Follow Us

New Update

RATING - ⭐ ⭐ 2/5*

7 Dogs Review Movie Talkies:

Adil and Bilal duo's latest South Arabian spy action drama, 7 Dogs, is a very mediocre affair. The film has an average script, let down by the poor efforts from the team. For an action thriller, it lacks almost everything, from thrill and pacing to style. It uses those dated templates that have been seen again and again, becoming more boring with each passing minute. You have seen the Mission Impossible and James Bond series—now just imagine them with low efforts, and your 7 Dogs is ready. Adil and Bilal take you on a world tour but hardly provide any chance to actually feel the excitement.

7 Dogs Story:

The film follows the story of an agent, Khalid, who is called for an important mission just ahead of his wedding. He begins with Ghali but is eventually forced to work with him to crack the syndicate. From the Indian Dog to the European Cartel Queen and finally the main Dog of the syndicate, the two find it difficult to track down the Pink Lady drug. Khalid has a personal trauma from childhood, whereas Ghali has his own secrets. One of the major differences between the two is the issue of trust. Will Khalid be able to trust Ghali? Will these two, along with two female officers, be able to carry out the mission successfully?

Like I said, 7 Dogs is a very routine film with outdated conflicts. The storytelling makes it even more boring, with animation effects adding a childish layer to the proceedings. You also have some imaginative sequences and special-effects scenes that look poorly executed. The screenplay has a pace, thankfully. The basic storyline is strictly average material, but the screenplay couldn't expand it well enough. It simply couldn't grasp the potential of the plot. You don't feel any connect with the characters, and that's the biggest flaw in the writing. For Bollywood lovers, there are two cameos—Salman Khan and Sanjay Dutt—and just look at how badly written their roles are. They are in Mumbai, but the locations don't look like Mumbai at all. The basic conviction is missing. The same happens throughout the film, even with the heist part that we have seen in over a dozen films already. That MI + 007 mix isn't good at all.

7 Dogs Cast:

The performances are also mediocre. Ahmed Ezz looks good in the beginning but then starts losing his grip on the character. Karim Abdel Aziz is a reverse case. He starts off dull and then becomes better in the later portion. The super gorgeous Monica Bellucci spreads the magic of her beauty once again. Tara Emad and Sandy Bella are also decent. Sayed Ragab and Naseer Qasabi are strictly okay, and the rest of the cast doesn't impress much either. Salman Khan and Sanjay Dutt's cameos, too, don't leave any impact.

Parents To 2 Children, Arjun Rampal & Gabriella Demetriades finally get married after 6 years –

7 Dogs Movie Review:

On the technical front, the film can be called a decent product. The cinematography is good, the music is bad, and the editing is dicey. The production design looks of lower quality considering the previous Hollywood works of Adil and Bilal, though it might generally be considered good for the Arabian film industry. Coming to the directors, Adil El Arbi and Bilall Fallah could have broken a norm with this one, but they played it too safely. 7 Dogs is neither thrilling nor exciting, so then by the hell is it called a spy action thriller? That's the major issue we have to deal with as an audience. Overall, it is a film made with low effort that, expectedly, delivers low results.
