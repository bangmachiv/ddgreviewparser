      Bhai Tera Star Hai Review: A Bumbling Comedy of Errors

      Cast:Raghav Juyal, Sanjay Kapoor, Barkha Singh, Vivan Bhathena, Niharika NM, Parvathy Omanakuttan, Niki Walia, Chandan Roy Sanyal
      Director:Vivek B Agrawal
      Genre:Comedy
      Duration:1 hours 45 minutes
      Quick take: Bhai Tera Star Hai is an ensemble comedy set over a single night in London. It chronicles the different misadventures the actors go through.

      Critic's rating 2.5/5

      Comedies set over a single day or night aren’t easy to make. You can either hit the bullseye or miss it completely. There’s no middle ground here. Classics of the genre, like After Hours (1985) or Dazed and Confused (1993) have thrived on controlled chaos, where every twist adds to the fun. Bhai Tera Star Hai tries hard to recreate the spirit of such madcap comedies but doesn’t have the legs to get there. Director Vivek B Agrawal is clearly in love with Priyadarshan and David Dhawan comedies from the 1990s and the film can be seen as his homage to his idols. Sadly, while the ingredients are all in place, the execution never quite comes together.

      The story follows Ajay Singh (Raghav Juyal), an aspiring Bollywood actor in London who believes stardom is just around the corner despite possessing far more confidence than talent. After losing 10,000 pounds in a cricket bet to the loud and intimidating club owner Fatty (Sanjay Kapoor), Ajay is given until sunrise to repay the debt. Desperate to save himself, he spins one lie after another, dragging his sister Natasha (Parvathy Omanakuttan), girlfriend Roshni (Niharika NM), Roshni's overbearing brother Sid (Vivaan Bhathena), and several others into an increasingly tangled web of misunderstandings. Naturally, everyone ends up under one roof for a climactic comedy of errors.

      On paper, it sounds like a perfect setup for a breezy entertainer. On screen, however, the film struggles to maintain momentum. Instead of building comic tension, it keeps piling on new characters and subplots every few minutes. The screenplay lacks the precision that a one-night narrative demands. Rather than letting situations generate humour organically, the film relies on constant shouting, frantic running and repetitive misunderstandings. Before long, the noise becomes exhausting.

      The nearly one-hour-45-minute runtime feels far longer than it actually is. Just when the story appears to be heading towards its inevitable conclusion, it awkwardly pauses for two full-fledged song-and-dance numbers, one a romantic track and the other a Bhangra routine. By then, the narrative has already run its course, making these additions feel unnecessary.

      Raghav Juyal throws himself wholeheartedly into Ajay's character. His physical comedy and comic timing are evident, and there are moments where his childlike overconfidence genuinely works. But the screenplay mistakes relentless energy for humour. Without quieter moments to balance his performance, Ajay often comes across as loud rather than lovable.

      Parvathy Omanakuttan is easily the film's emotional anchor. Natasha is perhaps the only character who behaves like a real person amid the surrounding madness. She brings warmth and restraint, making her scenes a welcome break from the chaos.

      Content creator Niharika NM, who has already made her debut in South film, makes her Bollywood debut here. She lands a few amusing one-liners but the writing gives her little opportunity to leave a lasting impression. Vivaan Bhathena plays the perpetually flustered Sid with conviction despite the absurdity of his track. Barkha Singh barely gets enough material to register.

      The film only truly comes alive when Chandan Roy Sanyal finally appears as Hamid Jalalabadi, a police officer who also happens to be Ajay's former acting guru. His scenes with Raghav Juyal are genuinely funny and inject the film with the kind of effortless humour it has been missing all along. Their chemistry finally delivers the laughs that the screenplay has been promising. If only the director had taken those sequences as a blueprint for the rest of the film, Bhai Tera Star Hai might have been a far more enjoyable affair.

      Sanjay Kapoor has fun playing the blustering Fatty and brings enough swagger to make the character watchable. As far as the rest of the film is concerned, most of the jokes fall flat. Cringeworthy one-liners and forced punchlines generate more groans than laughs, while the film repeatedly mistakes volume for wit. What with the subplot revolving around two paramedics? It clearly belonged to some medical comedy.

      There is the skeleton of an entertaining comedy buried somewhere inside Bhai Tera Star Hai. But weak writing, stretched plotting and loud performances prevent it from finding its rhythm. What should have been a fast-paced, laugh-out-loud night of mayhem instead becomes a drawn-out exercise in missed opportunities.
