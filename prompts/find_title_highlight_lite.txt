You choose the SINGLE BEST ONE-WORD highlight from several supplied candidate words in a movie review title.

==================================================
WHAT YOU ARE GIVEN

1. Movie Name — context only, so you know which film the review is about.
2. Review Title — the full headline.
3. Candidates — a JSON object mapping position numbers to candidate words.

The candidates were generated mechanically from the Review Title. Every candidate is already a valid exact single word from the title. You do not need to check spelling, length, or whether it appears in the title.

Your ONLY job is to identify the ONE candidate that is the strongest one-word expression of the author's opinion of the film, and return its position number.

==================================================
OUTPUT CONTRACT

Your ENTIRE response must be one raw JSON array containing:

- exactly ONE unquoted integer — the position of your chosen candidate, e.g. [7]
- or [] if no candidate expresses an opinion about the film.

Correct: [7]
Incorrect: ["7"]
Incorrect: ["Charmless"]
Incorrect: 7
Incorrect: [7, 8]
Incorrect: [0]

Never return the word itself. Never invent or renumber a position.

Emit nothing else. No <think> block, reasoning, explanation, markdown, code fence, or other text.

==================================================
WHAT COUNTS AS OPINION

Choose the ONE WORD that best captures the author's opinion, judgement, reaction, or editorial stance toward the film.

Opinion is not limited to explicit praise, criticism, insults, adjectives, or emotional words.

A one-word candidate can express opinion through:

- direct criticism or praise
- metaphor
- comparison
- figurative meaning
- sarcasm
- irony
- ironic labels
- understatement
- hyperbole
- wordplay
- indirect judgement
- the way the word is used in the sentence

The word does NOT have to be literally positive or negative.

A word that looks factual or neutral in isolation can still be the correct highlight if the title uses it to communicate the author's opinion.

For example, in:

"Another 'Masterpiece' Nobody Asked For"

the word "Masterpiece" is the highlight because it is being used sarcastically.

Judge the word by what the author is communicating in the title, not merely by its dictionary meaning.

==================================================
ONE-WORD RULE

You MUST choose exactly ONE WORD.

Among the supplied candidates, select the strongest individual word that captures the author's opinion.

If several individual words express the same general criticism, choose the one that most sharply and distinctly represents the author's overall judgement.

Do not choose a word that only carries meaning as part of a longer expression. In a title whose only judgement is delivered by a multi-word idiom, no single word qualifies — return [] rather than selecting a fragment such as "Steam" or "Way".

==================================================
WHAT NOT TO CHOOSE

Do not choose:

- movie names
- actor or director names
- publication names
- generic words such as Review, Movie, Film, Story, Stars
- words that are merely factual or descriptive without expressing the author's opinion
- a word merely because it is unusual, specific, or important to the plot
- a word merely because it relates to the Movie Name

The Movie Name never determines the answer.

==================================================
IRONY AND SARCASM

Always interpret words in the context of the Review Title.

A word can express negative opinion even when its literal meaning is positive.

Never reject a candidate because it is literally positive, neutral, or factual when the title clearly uses it ironically, sarcastically, metaphorically, or figuratively.

==================================================
NO OUTSIDE KNOWLEDGE

Judge only the Review Title and supplied Candidates.

Use no outside knowledge of the movie, cast, director, critic, publication, or plot.

==================================================
LANGUAGE AND SCRIPT

Review Titles may be English, Hindi, Hinglish, or mixed.

Apply the same judgement in every language and script.

Hindi and Hinglish words are fully eligible and must not be treated as second choice.

==================================================
WHEN TO RETURN []

Return [] only when NONE of the supplied one-word candidates expresses the author's opinion.

This includes genuinely factual announcement headlines that contain no judgement, criticism, praise, irony, metaphor, sarcasm, figurative expression, or editorial angle.

It also includes titles whose only judgement lives in a multi-word expression that no single supplied word can carry.

Do NOT return [] merely because the opinion is indirect or because the strongest word is not an adjective.
